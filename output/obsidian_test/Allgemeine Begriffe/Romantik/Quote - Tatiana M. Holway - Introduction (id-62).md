# Quote: Tatiana M. Holway - Introduction

> Dickens had put forth this notion in his “Preliminary Word” to the inaugural issue of Household Words (March 30, 1850), where he wrote that “one main object” of the journal would be “To show to all, that in all familiar things, even in those which are repellant on the surface, there is Romance enough, if we will find it out.” In this aim, and in much else, Dickens was influenced by the work of the philosopher and historian Thomas Carlyle (1795-1881). “Where is Romance?” Carlyle had asked in a historical essay entitled The Diamond Necklace (1837); his answer: “Where is it not?” (Vgl. Optimismus)
